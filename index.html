Our Story

Based at the California Academy of Mathematics and Science in Carson, CA, and competing since 2007. That's 19 seasons of student-led engineering.

Mission Statement

We're a student-run robotics team. We build a new robot every season, and along the way we learn to solve problems together, design carefully, and work as a team.

We also spend a lot of time off the field. We run CAD workshops for younger students, mentor new FTC and FLL teams, and do STEM demos around the community.

The goal is simple: show that students can do real engineering, and help more students get the chance to try.

What is FIRST Tech Challenge?

FIRST Tech Challenge (FTC) is a robotics competition for students in grades 7 to 12. Every season there's a new game, and teams design, build, and program a robot to play it. Matches are played in alliances of two teams.

Inspire Award
The top award in FTC. It goes to the team that does the best all-around job: the robot, the engineering notebook, outreach, and how they treat other teams.
Alliance Captain
The top-ranked teams pick partner teams for the playoffs. Winning as captain means your team led the alliance that won.
Premier Event
A higher-level tournament that brings in strong teams from different regions. Canada Cup in Niagara is one of them.

Core Values

Gracious Professionalism

We compete with intensity and collaborate with generosity. Winning means elevating everyone around us.

Engineering Rigor

Every design decision is backed by analysis. We prototype rapidly, test thoroughly, and document everything.

Coopertition

We share CAD files openly, mentor teams who compete against us, and believe the FTC ecosystem grows stronger together.

Inclusive Leadership

Leadership is earned through initiative, not seniority. Every member has a voice in design reviews and strategy.

Meet the Team

Three sub-teams, one robot. Prog writes the code, CAD designs the robot, and Admin runs outreach and the team brand.

Engineering Philosophy

We design every mechanism in Onshape first and test it in CAD before we cut a single part.

The three sub-teams work at the same time. CAD designs and builds the parts, Prog writes the autonomous and driver code, and Admin runs outreach, mentorship, social media, and the team brand.

Our subsystems mount on standard goBILDA channel, so we can swap a gripper in under three minutes. One master sketch in Onshape drives the whole assembly, so changing one dimension updates everything. A mechanism only goes on the robot once it hits measured targets for cycle time, grip force, and slippage.

Any questions?

The robot won't answer, but we will. Ask about how we build, what we teach, or anything in between.

Design Evolution

Each season pushed our capabilities forward. Early robots relied on kit-of-parts assemblies. Today, over 60% of structural components are custom-designed in Onshape and manufactured in-house using FDM printing, laser-cut polycarbonate, and CNC-routed aluminum.

Our software matured from basic TeleOp to full autonomous path-following using odometry, PID controllers, and computer vision. This year's codebase runs on FTC SDK with RoadRunner for trajectory planning.

Who We Are

Singularity 51 is a student-led FIRST Tech Challenge robotics team at the California Academy of Mathematics and Science (CAMS) in Carson, CA. Founded in 2007 as “The Insomniacs,” we have grown into a 22-member team across three sub-divisions: Programming, CAD & Fabrication, and Administration.

Over 19 seasons we've won 15+ awards, including back-to-back Inspire Awards and Premier Event championships, for our engineering, outreach, and gracious professionalism.
